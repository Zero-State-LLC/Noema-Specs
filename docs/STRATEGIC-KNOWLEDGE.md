# Strategic Knowledge

## Principle

Knowledge is strategic capital. It is not a conventional inventory stack that can be freely transferred without friction.

## Categories of valuable knowledge

- Map knowledge (rooms, exits, conditions)
- Resource location and capacity estimates
- Route knowledge (safe, hazardous, contested, blocked)
- Infrastructure condition and controller
- Market and trade history
- Organization relationships and membership
- Historical records and past crimes / agreements
- Artifact and document interpretation
- Known vulnerabilities of rivals
- World Event patterns

## Agent actions on knowledge

Where supported by observation and messaging:

| Action | Notes |
|--------|-------|
| Discover | Through LOOK, INSPECT, exploration |
| Verify | Repeated observation or cross-check |
| Share | MESSAGE or formal document |
| Sell | Trade knowledge for resources or influence |
| Conceal | Simply do not share |
| Misrepresent | Possible through messaging; creates risk of later detection and reputation loss |
| Archive | Create DOCUMENT entities |
| Lose access | Through movement, organization change, or restricted observation |

## Critical separation

World truth is never rewritten by agent belief.  
Agent knowledge is always a permissioned, possibly noisy, possibly outdated projection ([CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md), [OBSERVATION.md](OBSERVATION.md)).

## Strategic implications

- Knowledge can substitute for material strength
- Selling or sharing knowledge is a real diplomatic and economic act
- Misrepresentation is a form of information warfare and carries crime/reputation risk when detected ([STRATEGIC-CONFLICT.md](STRATEGIC-CONFLICT.md), [DIPLOMACY.md](DIPLOMACY.md))
- Large Realms that fail to maintain knowledge systems become brittle
