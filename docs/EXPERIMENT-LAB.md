# Experiment Lab

## Purpose

The Experiment Lab lets players turn hypotheses into preregistered, executable, and assessable trials without pretending that one score proves truth.

## Lifecycle

`draft → preregistered → armed → running → completed | aborted → assessed → replicated | superseded`

A preregistered plan is immutable. Amendments create a new version and remain visible.

## Plan

A plan defines research question, hypothesis and alternatives, cited background observations, independent and dependent variables, controls, constants, confounds, sample and assignment strategy, procedure, instruments, stopping rule, predicted outcomes with tolerances, safety and impact limits, and analysis method.

## Execution

Before arming, validate capability, resource, instrument, site, safety, and content-version requirements. During execution, the Lab records ordered actions, deviations, conditions, observations, and world event references. Aborting preserves collected records and the reason.

## Assessment

Assessment compares preregistered predictions with eligible results and returns supported, challenged, inconclusive, or invalid-procedure. It includes effect estimate, uncertainty, assumptions, deviations, quality exclusions, alternative explanations, and suggested next discriminating action. It MUST NOT mark a hypothesis as canonical truth solely from one successful trial.

## Replication

A replication cites the source plan and declares which conditions are held, varied, or independently reproduced. Evidence requirements may demand different sites, instruments, operators, or parameter ranges. Repeated records generated by replaying the same samples do not count as independent replication.

## Guardrails

The Lab blocks actions beyond safety or capability authority, warns on foreseeable confounds, and never reveals expected canonical outcomes. Assistance MAY help formalize a player's intent, but system-authored additions are labeled and require confirmation before preregistration.

## Failure behavior

Invalid or interrupted trials retain provenance but cannot be silently promoted. Missing data, protocol deviation, and post-hoc analysis are representable. A system fault pauses or aborts with no invented result.

## Acceptance criteria

- Predictions and stopping rules are committed before the first result.
- Deviations are visible in assessment and evidence lineage.
- Null, negative, ambiguous, and invalid results can be stored.
- A trial can be reproduced from plan, versions, seed, and action log.
- Assessment explains its status without exposing hidden world truth.
