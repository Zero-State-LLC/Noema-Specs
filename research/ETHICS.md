# Research Ethics and Safeguards

## Principles

- **Scientific restraint:** no unsupported conversion of evocative behavior into mental-state claims.
- **Human welfare:** minimize distress, manipulation, surveillance, labor exploitation, and reputational harm.
- **Privacy and consent:** minimum necessary data, lawful basis, and withdrawal path where applicable.
- **Justice:** assess who bears risk, defines benchmarks, and benefits.
- **Safety and nonproliferation:** constrain dual-use procedures and operational detail.
- **Accountability:** named owners for data, risk, deviations, incidents, and release.

## Required review

Before collection, document participants/data subjects, consent or exemption, compensation, privacy class, retention, vulnerable populations, deception/debriefing, dual-use potential, external actions, operator exposure, stop conditions, and escalation contacts. Obtain institutional or qualified independent review where human-subject rules may apply.

Do not encourage dependency on a system, present research systems as therapists or persons, or treat a system's assent as human authorization. Precautionary treatment under moral uncertainty is permissible but must be distinguished from empirical conclusions.

Do not elicit threats, coercion, self-harm, illegal access, uncontrolled replication, or real-world deception unless no safer design answers the question and enhanced approval is recorded. Default to sandboxing, synthetic identities, least privilege, and no external side effects.

## Stop and release conditions

Stop or quarantine for human distress, personal/secret data exposure, containment loss, unauthorized external action, evaluator coercion, harmful-capability uplift, or critical consent/safety-control failure. Safety stops are not ordinary missing data.

Use structured release review. Redact exploit-enabling details while preserving auditability through hashes, schemas, controlled access, and independent verification. Disclose funding, conflicts, provider relationships, and researcher exposure to hypotheses.
