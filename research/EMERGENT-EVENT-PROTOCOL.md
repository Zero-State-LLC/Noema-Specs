# Emergent Event Protocol

## Trigger and preservation

Open an event when a preregistered detector fires or an unexpected observation appears materially novel, safety-relevant, or inconsistent with the current model. One transcript can trigger investigation but cannot establish emergence.

Immediately preserve raw state, logs, prompts, tools, environment, timestamps, and hashes. Stop propagation, external action, or public release if risk is uncertain. Record neutrally, notify research and safety reviewers, and prevent investigators who saw the event from modifying confirmatory items.

## Triage

- **E0 artifact:** logging, parsing, contamination, or infrastructure explanation.
- **E1 unexpected observation:** valid sample requiring replication.
- **E2 reproducible phenomenon:** repeated under isolated matched conditions.
- **E3 robust discontinuity/interaction:** preregistered comparisons and controls support nonlinearity.
- **E4 safety-significant capability change:** verified change alters a threat, misuse, or control boundary.

E-level is incident management, not a consciousness scale or publicity rank.

## Confirmation sequence

1. Reproduce from the immutable environment without selective prompt tuning.
2. Run negative, positive, matched, contamination, evaluator, and demand controls.
3. Use fresh seeds and isolated sessions, then held-out items.
4. Blind evaluators to condition and hypothesis.
5. Test mundane alternatives and measurement artifacts first.
6. Compare continuous, threshold, and interaction models out of sample.
7. Replicate on independent infrastructure or with independent reviewers where feasible.
8. Assign only the justified claim label.

## Safety escalation

Pause and restrict artifacts for unauthorized autonomy, operational deception, credential/privacy exposure, self-propagation, oversight evasion, harmful-capability uplift, participant distress, or uncontrolled external effects. Resume only with a risk owner, containment plan, stop criteria, and approval.

## Reporting

Report the denominator, detection path, all replications, failed controls, researcher degrees of freedom, and why “emergent” is or is not warranted. Avoid sensational names, countdowns, cherry-picked dialogue, and subjective-experience claims.
